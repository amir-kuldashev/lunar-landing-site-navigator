# Demo script

Two versions: a 30-second cut for the submission video (the limit to confirm in `SUBMISSION.md`), and a
90-second version for live judging. Record at 1440 px wide or more, so the Moon and the readouts sit side by side.

## 30-second video

| Time | On screen | Say or caption |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Page loads; camera flies to the Shackleton ridge | "Landing at the Moon's south pole? You need sunlight for power and a view of Earth for radio." |
| 4–10 s | Readout sentence and the Sun and Earth cards | "Pick a site and a date. The Navigator tells you if you have both, and for how long." |
| 10–17 s | Press Start in the top bar; Sun skims the skyline, Earth dips under it, timeline cursor moves | "At the pole both hover at the horizon. Terrain decides everything." |
| 17–24 s | Drag the schedule-slip slider to "21 days later"; delta cards change | "Your launch slips three weeks. See instantly what you lose." |
| 24–30 s | Pin two scenarios; compare table | "Compare sites and dates side by side. Checked against NASA JPL Horizons." |

## 90-second live walkthrough

1. **The problem (10 s).** A lander needs the Sun and a line to Earth. At the south pole both sit within a few
   degrees of the horizon.
2. **One site, one date (20 s).** Shackleton connecting ridge. Read the plain-language sentence, then the
   cards: Sun about 1.4° up, Earth below the skyline, countdown to Earthrise.
3. **Why it is hard (20 s).** Point at the sky view: the terrain skyline, the Sun's dotted path grazing it,
   Earth's small loop crossing it. Press Start for a few seconds. Hover the sky view to read a point.
4. **The schedule slips (20 s).** Slide to 21 days later. Read one delta card aloud. This is the question
   mission teams actually face.
5. **Compare (10 s).** Pin this scenario, switch to another site, pin again. Read the table.
6. **Trust (10 s).** 1,060 Sun and Earth angles checked against JPL Horizons, largest difference 0.0001°.
   Say what the tool does not do: surface hazards.
